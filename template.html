Macro regime · methodology & sourcing notes

  • Ported from Global Macro Regime (QuantitativeAlpha, TradingView) — same trend-cross signal logic (EMA30/EMA60, ATR-scaled margin) and sticky-regime aggregation across cross-asset signals, run in Python against open data instead of TradingView's feed.
  • Credit spreads, breakevens and US Treasury yields come straight from FRED — more faithful than the original's ETF-ratio workaround for these series.
  • Equities, FX, commodities, VIX, MOVE and 3M correlation come from Yahoo Finance's public chart feed. 3 proxies & 1 substitution — hover ⓘ in the table below.
  • 3M Implied Correlation (COR3M) was dropped: Yahoo carries only a live snapshot, no history, and a fabricated substitute would defeat the point of an open-data source.

Backtest · as-published data

Level and 52-week MA use today's NFCI history, which the Chicago Fed re-estimates every week. Weekly changes from May 2011 are as first published (ALFRED vintages), because that is what a weekly rule actually trades on — today's smoothed history has had no weekly move above 0.02 since March 2023. Earlier weekly changes come from today's history. The >0.02 rule keeps its position until a weekly move clears ±0.02. Backtest: SPY total return when on, 3-month T-bills when off, positions moved the Thursday after each release, 5bp per switch.

Modelled on Lion Global Investors' Risk Barometer table, whose method isn't published — this is a free-data reconstruction, not their numbers. Each input is scored against its own last 3 years (z-score, capped at ±1, signed so positive = risk-on); a pillar averages its inputs and the Risk Index averages the 8 pillars, with ±0.33 as the colour lines. Proxies: the Fed's Weekly Economic Index stands in for the Citi surprise index, S&P 500 vs its 10-week average for trend-follower positioning, and HYG/LQD/EMB vs IEF for credit spreads. It reads stress as it happens rather than forecasting it: the S&P has on average done better after risk-off weeks, because stress tends to peak near market lows.
