Macro regime · methodology & sourcing notes

  • Ported from Global Macro Regime (QuantitativeAlpha, TradingView) — same trend-cross signal logic (EMA30/EMA60, ATR-scaled margin) and sticky-regime aggregation across cross-asset signals, run in Python against open data instead of TradingView's feed.
  • Credit spreads, breakevens and the US 2Y yield come from FRED, as in the original; the US 10Y uses Cboe's ^TNX for full daily ranges.
  • Equities, FX, commodities, VIX and MOVE come from Yahoo Finance's public chart feed; 3M implied correlation (COR3M) from Cboe's daily history file. 3 proxies & 1 substitution — hover ⓘ in the table below.
  • Each signal runs on its own market's trading days before being carried onto the S&P 500 calendar, as TradingView's request.security does.

Level and 52-week MA use today's NFCI history, which the Chicago Fed re-estimates every week. Weekly changes from May 2011 are as first published (ALFRED vintages), because that is what a weekly rule actually trades on — today's smoothed history has had no weekly move above 0.02 since March 2023. Earlier weekly changes come from today's history. The >0.02 rule keeps its position until a weekly move clears ±0.02. Backtest: SPY total return when on, 3-month T-bills when off, positions moved the Thursday after each release, 5bp per switch.

Macro pressure · early warning

The average of the barometer's slow macro pillars (economic surprise, liquidity, rates, FX), drawn under the gauge above. When it is weak (below −0.33) while the gauge is still calm, market stress has tended to follow — red marks on the chart. A watch signal, not a sell signal: about half of these episodes came to nothing, and the 2018 Q4 and 2025 selloffs came without one.

Does it read stress as it happens?
S&P 500 after each reading

Built to measure risk as it happens, from fast market prices only: VIX, the VIX/VIX3M term structure and MOVE (volatility); high-yield and investment-grade bond ETFs vs Treasuries, total return, measured below their 52-week high (credit); and the S&P 500 below its 52-week high and vs its 50-day average (equity trend). Each input is scored weekly against its whole history since 2000 with a robust z-score (median and median absolute deviation, as drawdowns and VIX are one-sided and fat-tailed), signed so positive = risk-on, divided by 3 and capped at ±1; the three blocks are equal-weighted. Tiers: risk on above +0.15, risk off below −0.15, stress below −0.40. Scoring against the full history rather than a rolling window keeps a calm spell from turning a small wobble into a crisis reading. A coincident gauge, not a forecast: like stress itself, its worst readings tend to come near market lows.

A broader, slower read than the gauge above: it adds macro pillars (economic surprise, liquidity, rates, FX, gold), and scoring against a rolling 3-year window makes it more jumpy — it flagged about half of the big stress weeks since 2013, against nine in ten for the gauge. Each input is scored against its own last 3 years (z-score, capped at ±1, signed so positive = risk-on); a pillar averages its inputs and the Risk Index averages the 8 pillars, with ±0.33 as the colour lines. Proxies: the Fed's Weekly Economic Index stands in for the Citi surprise index, S&P 500 vs its 10-week average for trend-follower positioning, and HYG/LQD/EMB vs IEF for credit spreads. It reads stress as it happens rather than forecasting it: the S&P has on average done better after risk-off weeks, because stress tends to peak near market lows.

What each regime has meant · next 20 sessions, all markets pooled

The regimes describe risk, not direction. Since 2001, average 20-session returns have been no better after risk-on readings than after risk-off ones; markets tend to rebound after selloffs. What changes is the range of outcomes: volatility and the worst 5% of returns are much larger in the risk-off regimes.

Commitments of Traders positioning

—

Large speculators (non-commercial traders) in the CFTC legacy futures-only report. Net = long minus short contracts. Index: where today's net position sits between its low (0) and high (100) of the last 1, 3 and 5 years of weekly reports. Signal uses the 3-year index: 80 or above bullish, 20 or below bearish. Trend: the 3-year index over the last 26 weeks. Positions are as of Tuesday and published the following Friday. Extreme readings show crowding; they have often come near turning points, so read them as positioning, not as a forecast.

Backtest · futures after each reading4

Layout modelled on WallStreetCourier's Big Picture page. Health scores, regimes and positioning indices here are our own, built from daily closes (Yahoo Finance) and the CFTC public reporting API; their ~40-indicator scores and Smart Money Flow Index are proprietary and not reproduced. Health per horizon averages 5–7 price-only inputs: price vs moving averages, moving-average slopes, returns, the fit and up-day share of the trend, RSI and position in the trading range. Trend inputs are scaled by each market's own volatility so that all 29 markets score on one scale. No constituent breadth data is used. CSI 300 uses the ASHR ETF and Emerging Markets the EEM ETF.

  1. Smart money flow proxy: our own construction, not WallStreetCourier's index. The classic Smart Money Index subtracts the first 30 minutes' move and adds the last hour's. Yahoo keeps hourly bars for only two years, so we use the Dow's move from the previous close to 10:30 (including the overnight gap) and its move over the last 90 minutes. The line shown under the Dow is net of its average daily move, because the morning leg includes the overnight gain, which is positive on average and makes the raw line drift down. Daily values are now archived in the repository, so the history will grow past two years.
  2. Breadth proxy: share of today's S&P 500 members (Wikipedia list) and Nasdaq-100 members (Nasdaq's list) above their 50- and 200-day averages, from Yahoo daily closes. Past readings use today's members, so stocks that left the index are missing (survivorship bias); a stock counts only once it has 50 or 200 days of history.
  3. Regime backtest: each market held when the rule is on and in 3-month T-bills when off. The position is set at one close and earns the next day's return; 5bp per switch. Index levels exclude dividends for both the rule and buy & hold. Medians across the 29 markets from each market's first scored day (2001 for most). The health formula and regime zones were set before this test and not tuned to it, but it is still one in-sample parameter set.
  4. CoT backtest: front-month continuous futures from Yahoo, which include jumps at each contract roll. US dollar index uses the spot index and VIX futures the spot VIX; spring wheat and 3M SOFR have no price series and are left out. Entry at the close of the first trading day after Friday's publication. Edge is the average forward move against the contract's own average move, in standard deviations, so contracts with different volatility can be pooled. Speculator positions in Treasury futures largely reflect hedge funds' basis trades (short futures against long bonds), not a view on rates, so read the rates row with care.
